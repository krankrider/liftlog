# Program v5 — Max-Efficiency Hypertrophy, Upper/Lower, 4 days

Goal: hypertrophy and looks. Priorities: chest, side delts, traps, and from v4 also lats for
the V-taper. Built on Andy Galpin's principles: minimum effective dose, training close to
failure, rep-range variety. The v3 PDF next to this file has the exercise guide with cues
and search terms; the session tables below supersede its tables. v4 was agreed on 2026-10-01,
v5 on 2026-10-09.

Week: Mon Upper A · Tue Lower A · Wed rest · Thu Upper B · Fri Lower B · Sat and Sun rest.
Days can shift. Keep at least one rest day between the two upper sessions.

## Principles

- **Effort.** Most sets at RIR 0–2. RIR is reps left in the tank when you stop; 0 is failure.
  The single biggest lever.
- **Rep spectrum.** 5–8 recruits big motor units through load, 15–20 through fatigue. Both
  every week.
- **Volume.** 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week. More is fatigue, not growth.
- **Progression.** Double progression: add reps inside the range. When every set hits the top
  of the range, add weight next time and restart at the bottom. Also add weight when every set
  was RIR 3 or easier: the weight is too light to count as a hard set. The app applies these
  rules itself: it fills in +2.5 kg and the bottom of the range. Below the range two sessions
  in a row at the same weight, it fills in about 10% less (rounded to 2.5 kg). One bad session
  only gets a badge. Deload weeks keep the weight.
- **Deload.** Every 5–6 weeks cut volume about 40% for one week, or sooner when reps stall two
  weeks running or joints complain. In the first blocks a fixed schedule is premature.
  In the app, a deload week keeps the weights and cuts sets to about 60% (3 → 2, 4 → 2, 2 → 1).
- **Rest.** Compounds 2–3 min, isolation 60–90 s. Cutting rest on compounds cuts reps, which
  cuts total tension. Whole session under 60 min.
- **Warm-up.** 3–5 min general. Ramp-up only on the first compound: empty bar ×10, 50% ×5,
  70% ×3, 85% ×1, about 1 min between. Later compounds get at most one feeler set, isolation
  none. No static stretching before lifting.
- **Tempo.** 2–3 s controlled down, explosive up. A slow eccentric is free tension; a slow
  concentric is just fatigue.
- **Order.** Weak points first while fresh. On Upper B, shrugs are already second.
- **Supersets.** A1/A2 and B1/B2 pairs are done back to back, then rest 90 s (arms) or 2 min
  (compound pairs). Opposing muscles do not steal from each other.

## Changes from v4 and why

1. **Neck parked.** The gym has no neck gear. Neck Curl, Neck Extension and Lateral Neck
   Flexion are out. Logged neck sets stay in the saved data; bring the same names back and
   their history picks up again. Each lower day is about 3 min shorter.
2. **Abs added.** Squats and deadlifts load the abs but do little to grow them. Direct work
   thickens them, so they show sooner when lean. Cable Crunch 3 × 10–15 at RIR 0–1 takes the
   neck's A2 slot next to calves on both lower days: 6 sets a week, no extra time. It is
   loaded in normal weight steps, so the add-weight rule works as on any other lift.
3. **No heavy oblique work.** Thicker obliques widen the waist, against the V-taper.

## Changes from v3 and why

1. **Lats were the hole.** Six sets a week against nine for chest and roughly eighteen
   effective for triceps. Back width is what makes the V-taper. Pull-ups went to 4 sets on
   Upper A and a lat pulldown at 12–15 was added to Upper B.
2. **Farmer's carries dropped.** Lowest growth per minute on the sheet. Traps keep 4 shrugs on
   Upper B and gained 3 trap-bar shrugs on Lower B straight off the loaded bar, so traps are
   now hit twice a week with no setup time.
3. **Trap bar only, 3 × 5–8.** Conventional deadlift was the most fatigue per unit of growth in
   the plan. The trap bar hits quads and traps harder and has a rep range, so the progression
   rule applies. Bulgarian split squat also got a range, 8–12 (v3 had a fixed 10).
4. **Second compound paired with its antagonist.** Incline DB with pull-ups on A, chest press
   with rows on B. Same logic v3 already used for arms. Saves about 5 min per upper day.
5. **Calves and neck as a superset.** Lateral neck flexion 3 → 2 per side. Neck still gets 14
   sets. Saves 6–7 min per lower day.
6. **Cable laterals 3 → 4 on Upper B.** Priority muscle, 90 seconds.
7. **Incline DB 3 → 4 on Upper A.** Makes "extra chest volume" true and evens the pairing.

Optional, not adopted: 2 × 15–20 cable fly at the end of Upper A if chest should also get the
high-rep range (back now has it through the pulldown).

## RIR: making it objective (from v3)

1. **Failure calibration.** Week 1, take the last set of each isolation exercise to true
   failure and note the reps. Next session, stopping 2 short of that number at the same weight
   is RIR 2. Repeat once per mesocycle.
2. **Bar speed.** On compounds the last 2–3 reps before failure slow down involuntarily. All
   reps the same speed is RIR 4+. Last rep grinding is RIR 0–1.
3. **The log.** Stuck at the same weight and reps for two weeks means you are already near
   RIR 1–2. The numbers know before the feeling does.

## Outside the gym (from v3)

- Protein 1.6–2.2 g per kg bodyweight per day. Hit the floor, fill the rest with what you can
  actually eat.
- Lean surplus: weekly-average bodyweight up 0.25–0.5 kg per week. Weigh in the same
  conditions, morning after bathroom before food, and only read the weekly line. The app has
  a bodyweight field per session for this.
- Creatine monohydrate 5 g every day, no loading. Caffeine 3–6 mg/kg 30–45 min before lifting,
  never within about 10 h of bed. Sleep outranks both.
- Carbs are not a lever once protein and calories are matched. v3 cites a 2026 Henselmans
  meta-analysis in Sports Medicine that could not be verified; the 2022 Henselmans review in
  Nutrients supports the same conclusion.
